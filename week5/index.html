Spiral of Seconds

Week 5

I started by brainstorming different ways to communicate time without using a traditional clock. My early sketches used simple shapes that would gradually build into a complete form as seconds, minutes, and hours passed, but they still felt too similar to a circular clock. Inspired by Eliza Pratt’s dandelions, I experimented with adding a new line every second so the form would grow over time. The first version looked more like a structured radar, so I changed the lines to expand outward in a spiral, making the seconds feel more dynamic. I kept the minutes and hours as circles moving along inner orbits, which I am still exploring ways to make more abstract.

I referenced the p5.js documentation for functions such as hour(), minute(), second(), map(), random(), and randomSeed(). An LLM helped me work through the calculations for positioning elements using angles, sin() and cos(), and for developing the expanding spiral structure. I also used it to experiment with HSB values to create the softer randomized pastel color palette I wanted. I then adjusted values such as the angle spacing, radius increase, line length, stroke weight, saturation, and brightness through experimentation to get closer to the visual result I had in mind.
