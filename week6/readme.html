Spiral of Seconds

Week 6

For this week’s sketch, I continued developing my abstract clock from last week by focusing on adding more movement and making the different elements feel more connected. Looking back at my previous design, I felt that the hour and minute orbs looked too separate from the seconds spiral, so I decided to replace them with two additional spirals. Instead of having circles move along fixed orbits, the new spirals gradually grow as the minutes and hours pass, creating a more unified visual. I used an LLM to help me work through the calculations for the growing spirals and experimented with their angles, spacing, and thickness to make sure they could overlap without becoming too crowded.

I also wanted to make the seconds spiral feel more animated rather than just having new lines appear every second. Referencing our class materials and p5.js documentation, I used sin() to add a subtle breathing motion to the overall spiral and individual oscillations to the line lengths. I also used an LLM to help me understand and experiment with easing so that each new line would gradually extend outward instead of appearing immediately. To add another layer of movement, I created five small dots that orbit around the center and gently change size using sine waves. I also experimented with setInterval() to cycle through different purple color palettes every five seconds and added a background that switches between dark purple and white every 30 seconds. Throughout the process, I adjusted the animation speed, spiral spacing, line lengths, and colors to make the overall composition feel more balanced and visually connected.
